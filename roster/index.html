SwimOutlet Team Stores · roster upload · product exercise response

The roster is not admin work. It is the machine that makes the store work — and the page sells it as a coupon.

A roster tells the store which swimmers exist, which group they are in, what gear that group requires, what sizes to collect and who to remind before a deadline. Membership and the 35% code are byproducts of that job being done: we earn accounts because a team got outfitted, not the other way round. The page states the byproduct and never states the function.

What your data corrected

A large share of first attempts fail, there is a multi-day gap between store created and roster live, and roster issues are a top support driver. Admins are trying and losing — a different problem from admins not being persuaded. So upload repair ranks first here, and the copy inversion became a cheap fast-follow rather than the headline.

Scope: Dashboard and Roster, deliberately · August 2026 · companion prototype covers the near-term column end to end

Part 2a

The page, as an admin with a TeamUnify export sees it

Eight findings on the live page. Click a pin or a card. Pink means the same thing here as in the prototype: something I would change.

1Inverted

The pitch is inverted

The banner sells a membership tier and a discount code. It never says what a roster does. Membership is what we earn once the store can do its job.

How I'd validate it Ask growth whether this copy is tested or shipped, then A/B job-first against perk-first on the same banner.

2Confusing

"Create a New Roster" has no stated purpose

A team with no roster is offered a second one, and the obvious shortcut for a returning team — last season's roster — is not offered at all.

How I'd validate it Session replay for new stores: how many click it, and what they do next.

3Missing

Add Member accepts a member who can never be reached

The modal saves a member with no email, silently creating a row that cannot receive the benefit the banner just promised. Nothing marks the field as required.

How I'd validate it Share of existing roster rows with no family email; tickets reading "my families never got the code".

4Confusing

Four date fields, no stated purpose or requirement

No indication of whether they block the upload, what they drive downstream, or what to do when the schedule is not set yet in August.

How I'd validate it Three coach interviews: is the season calendar even known at store setup?

5Confusing

Three surfaces, three vocabularies, no required marks

The grid says Athlete First Name and Family Email; the template says Account First Name and Email; Add Member asks for Swimmer Name; the help article uses a fourth set. An admin who hits an error has no stable term to search on.

How I'd validate it Support ticket review: which field names admins use to describe what broke.

6Missing

No mapping, no preview, no partial success

If headers do not match, the only path is reshaping the file by hand in Excel. Nothing shows what we understood before committing, and nothing says 300 of 312 rows were fine.

How I'd validate it Validation failures by class, plus the 30-file audit.

7Missing

Only two of six source systems are addressed

Teams on Hy-Tek, SwimCloud, SportsEngine and parent-maintained Sheets are the population most likely to fail, and the page speaks only to the two exports that already work.

How I'd validate it Attachment audit segmented by source system.

8Confusing

The guidance sits after the moment it is needed

The three how-to guides are below the upload button and below the fold. An admin who needed to prepare her file differently learns that after she has already failed.

How I'd validate it Scroll depth on the Roster page and click-through on the guide links.

What works, and is worth keeping

  • The empty state is honest, offers two paths rather than one, and names TeamUnify and SwimTopia explicitly. An admin holding one of those exports learns in a glance that she is in the right place.
  • The benefit is stated where the work is asked for, not buried in a help centre — the framing is wrong, but the placement is right.
  • Add Member exists, so a one-swimmer correction never needs a file.

Part 2b

What validation will choke on, and what to do about each

Note on inputs: I worked from the SwimOutlet template and from what exports out of the systems named in the brief actually look like; these are the classes I would confirm first against roster_sample.csv. Even the clean template contains three of them — a trailing space in an athlete first name, an apostrophe inside an email address, and a parent surname spelled differently from the athlete surname in the same row.

The rule I applied. Silently fix only what has exactly one correct answer. Fix and confirm anything where I am changing meaning. Flag anything only a human can decide. Hard-reject only when no row can be formed at all, or when importing would breach policy. And never make the admin start over: fixing happens in place.

Part 3

Target-state upload experience

Three steps, always the same three: drop the file → review what we understood → confirm. The review step is the product; everything else is plumbing. Each scenario below is clickable in the prototype.

First upload, brand-new team

  • The page leads with what the roster does for the season, with membership and the code as the second line, and names the three fields we need before she opens anything.
  • One drop zone accepting CSV, XLSX and the named platform exports; the template download demoted to a fallback link.
  • Parsing happens in the flow, so she lands on the review grid seeing her own swimmers' names — the moment the tool starts to feel trustworthy.
  • Commit and invite are separated: nothing reaches families until she chooses, and the benefit clock does not start on a roster she is still editing.
Try the upload flow →

Column mapping when the file does not match

  • Auto-map from the alias dictionary built in Part 1, each mapping shown as a best guess with a one-click override and a "not in my file" option.
  • Unmapped optional fields proceed; unmapped required fields block the commit and say which field and why.
  • Mappings save per team and per detected source, so the second upload never costs what the first did.
Load a TeamUnify sample and watch it map →

Partial failures: 300 good rows, 12 bad

  • Import the 300. Refusing the whole file over 4% of rows is the behaviour that sends people to support.
  • The 12 land in a fix queue on the roster, editable in place, grouped by problem class — twelve missing emails are one task, not twelve.
  • A downloadable file of just the failed rows, in the shape we accept, for whoever would rather work in Excel or forward it to a team parent.
  • Re-importing is idempotent, so the safest instinct — try again — is safe.

Duplicates and returning-season re-uploads

  • Match key: athlete first name, last name and date of birth or age, scoped to the team. Family email is a secondary signal only — siblings share it.
  • A re-upload shows a diff before anything commits: X new, Y changed, Z on the roster but not in this file. The Z group gets an explicit choice — leave or archive. Never silent deletion.
  • Benefits are idempotent per account: no second membership year, no second code, and three re-uploads do not send three emails to the same parent.
Try Duplicate for a returning season →

Mid-season changes

  • Add Member for one swimmer, and a shareable enrolment link for the rest — sent by email or text, families enter their own details. The only design here that removes the file from the coach's desk entirely.
  • New members inherit the benefit on join, with status visible per row: enrolled, invited, declined. Today that column cannot distinguish those states.

Three imports I would borrow from

Stripe. The dry run: nothing commits until you have seen a summary, and failures come back as a file you can fix and resubmit.

Flatfile (and Airtable). Mapping and correction inside a grid in the flow, so the file never reopens in Excel; mappings remembered.

Mailchimp. Dedupe on a stable key, with every automatic decision explained in one plain sentence.

The copy change outranking the engineering is the point, not an artifact of the math. It is the cheapest item on the board and it sits upstream of everything else: an admin weighing whether this is worth an evening is being sold a $5 membership rather than a team that shows up to the first meet in the right suits. That is a sequencing error in the pitch, and sequencing errors are free to fix. It is also the score I would most expect the group to argue with — which is what the scores are for.

Three solutions that get discussed as one

A · in the near term

Tolerant import with an editable review

Solves: they have the data, in the wrong shape. Deterministic, cheap, and what the prototype demonstrates.

B · later, gated

Image ingest

Solves: they have no digital file at all — a paper sign-up sheet, a screenshot. Different population, different technology, different risk profile.

C · later, the bet

Roster sync with the team-management platform

Solves: the transfer itself, and the fact that a roster goes stale. Not a better importer — no import at all.

The bigger swing is cheaper than it sounds

SwimOutlet and SwimTopia have been partnered since January 2022 — teams already embed their SwimOutlet Team Store into their SwimTopia site through a snippet, and that integration has been extended once since. The relationship, the contract and a technical touchpoint exist. This proposal runs the same pipe the other way: store content flows into SwimTopia today, and the roster would flow back out. That is an amendment and a sync job, not business development from zero.

Sequencing follows: SwimTopia first, where there is a partner and a shared incentive — their own marketing sells the partnership on saving parent volunteers time, and roster re-entry is the time it has not saved yet. TeamUnify is a separate, harder conversation with no agreement I can find, and bundling the two into one estimate is how this gets mis-sized. What I would verify first: whether roster data is already in scope of that agreement. Someone in the room knows.

The one I would put on the wall: first-attempt success rate. It is the failure the brief names, it drives the support cost, and it is the only one of these an admin experiences directly.

Instrumentation

Ships in week one on the existing flow, so the redesign has a baseline. Events: upload_started, file_parsed (source guess, rows, columns), mapping_auto (matched, unmatched), mapping_overridden (field), validation_error (class, affected rows), rows_committed, rows_quarantined, fix_applied (class), invite_sent, member_activated. The error class from Part 2b is a first-class dimension, so within a week we can rank causes instead of debating them. Log file shape — header names and counts — not file contents.

Would I A/B test it?

Not the flow. The population is small, seasonal and close to one-shot per team, so there is no path to a powered test inside 8 weeks — and holding half of new teams on a path we know fails, during the exact weeks they onboard, has a real cost in support load and goodwill. Instead: instrument, then pre/post segmented by source platform, a 10% canary watching error and abandonment rates, and five moderated sessions on real files before we ship.

Yes to two narrow tests. The banner — job-first against the current perk-first copy, one variable, a real hypothesis, and the losing variant costs nothing. And the invite email to families, where volume reads a result quickly.

Sequencing: three ships inside the 8-week horizon

The horizon is 8 weeks to first meaningful ship, but nothing here needs to wait that long. Three increments, each usable on its own, each measurable against the week-one baseline.

Where AI changes this estimate, and where it does not

Two of those weeks look different than they would have two years ago, and one of them is week one. Clustering a season of support tickets into a ranked error taxonomy, proposing an alias dictionary from a few hundred real header rows, and generating a synthetic corpus of malformed files to test the parser against are hours of work rather than days — and the test corpus is normally the slowest part of building tolerant parsing. Front-end scaffolding compresses similarly: the companion prototype, including mapping, validation and partial import, was built in an evening.

What does not compress: the privacy review on a family-facing page collecting minors' names, five moderated sessions with real coaches, QA against real files where a wrong email fails silently, the partnership conversation, and the season itself. Those are why the plan keeps two weeks of slack rather than promising a week-four finish.

The more interesting use is in the product, not the pipeline. A deterministic alias dictionary will never anticipate every header a parent's spreadsheet contains, and a model is a good fallback mapper for exactly that case. The disposition table in Part 2b is already the governance frame for it: a model may propose, a human confirms, and a model never silently fixes. Anything a model guessed lands in category (b), never (a) — which is what makes model-assisted mapping safe to ship on a file that decides whether a family ever hears from us.

Risks I would flag on day one

  • Season timing. Upload clusters at season start. Shipping in September catches this year's cohort; slipping past it means waiting a year. That is why the ordering front-loads parsing over polish.
  • The shared designer. One grid carries this release. If design capacity slips, weeks 4–5 are the critical path, not the parser.
  • Support workflow. Agents fix files by hand today. If they cannot work the new fix queue, the tickets do not go away, they change shape. They are users of this release too.
  • What I still do not know. Whether the cliff is before upload rather than during it. If most teams never reach the Roster page, this plan is aimed at the wrong screen and onboarding takes its place — which is exactly what week one is for.
